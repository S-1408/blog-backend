I have a drawer full of planners that begin on a Monday.

Each one contains a handful of very ambitious weeks and then, somewhere around the page marked “new month,” an abrupt silence. The plans weren’t bad. They were just plans for someone who apparently had no groceries to buy, no tired afternoons, and a limitless supply of fresh starts.

This year, I’m trying something smaller.

## A plan that fits inside a day

Instead of asking what I wanted to become this year, I started asking what I wanted an ordinary Wednesday to feel like.

Not a perfect Wednesday. The real one: a little busy, probably, maybe a bit grey outside. The kind of day that doesn’t make the highlight reel.

The answer was surprisingly simple. I wanted enough time to eat lunch sitting down. A walk before it got dark. One small stretch of the day that belonged to something I cared about.

So that’s what I started protecting. Lunch, a loop around the block, and twenty quiet minutes with a notebook. Not every day. Just often enough to notice when I missed them.

## Leave space in the margins

Planning can be a kind of hope. It says: the future is still something you can take part in.

But a useful plan has room for the person who has to live it. Room for a slower morning. Room for a phone call that runs long. Room for the unexpected good thing you wouldn’t have thought to schedule.

I’m learning to make plans that leave something out on purpose. Not because I’ve given up, but because I want to be there when the day does something different.

A smaller kind of plan feels less like a promise to become someone new. More like a note left for the person I already am: there’s a little room here. You can put something meaningful in it.
