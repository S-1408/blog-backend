The window seat is a good place to practice not being in charge.

The train leaves whether or not I’ve finished the sentence in my head. The city rearranges itself into smaller and smaller shapes. Someone else has worked out the route. For forty minutes, my only job is to be carried.

That is not my usual approach to time.

## The scenery between destinations

When I was younger, I thought the journey was mostly the inconvenience between the important parts. Get there, do the thing, come back.

Now I’m suspicious of any idea that says the in-between doesn’t count. So much of a life happens there: in waiting rooms, on a platform, in the quiet minute before someone picks up the phone.

This ride has a bridge where the water opens up underneath. I’ve crossed it a hundred times. I still look.

Today the river is the color of an old spoon. A cyclist on the path looks up at the train. Someone has left a red scarf on the bench. None of it asks for anything from me.

## A small collection

I’ve started collecting details the way I used to collect souvenirs. Not to prove I went somewhere, just to remember what was already there.

The sound of a door closing two cars away. An old woman reading the same page twice. Sunlight finding the exact corner of an empty seat.

I write these down in the margins of whatever I’m carrying. They don’t add up to a conclusion. They aren’t trying to.

Maybe the point is simply this: the window seat gives the world a frame, and the frame makes me look. For a little while, I don’t need to go anywhere else.
